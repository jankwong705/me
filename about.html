Bio

My name is Jan Kwong, and I am pursuing an M.S. in Computer Science at UC San Diego. My work focuses on machine learning and software engineering.

As a Graduate Teaching Assistant, I lead weekly discussion sections of around 40 students in Intro to Research Methods, guiding them through study design, statistical validation, and analysis. Earlier, I tutored roughly 200 students across the courses Theory of Computation and Advanced Data Structures, holding office hours and giving detailed feedback on their work. Teaching has sharpened how I explain complex ideas and made mentorship a core part of how I work.

At the UCSD LASR Lab, I co-authored a paper benchmarking OpenAI's Whisper against 75 human transcribers, implementing the Python script to compute word error rates and helping surface where models still fall short of humans on isolated words. I've since extended that curiosity into multi-agent LLM systems and performance analysis for research targeting systems conferences.

Most recently, I pretrained picoLM (a 98M-parameter transformer, from scratch in PyTorch), built a retrieval-augmented Q&A pipeline that reached 0.93 correctness under a strict token budget, and reverse-engineered macOS binaries to uncover a privacy vulnerability in Apple Intelligence.

Across teaching, research, and building, I gravitate toward work that is both rigorous, practical, and innovative. I'm excited to keep working across all three.
